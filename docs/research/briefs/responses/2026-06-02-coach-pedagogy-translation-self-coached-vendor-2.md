# Research Memo: Do Athlete-Set Session Goals and Forward-Looking Outcome Promises Survive Self-Administered App Delivery?

## TL;DR

- **Technique A (athlete-set per-session goal) partially translates under specific framings (moderate confidence):** a *process/learning* goal that is user-set and reviewed at session end retains a small genuine cognitive effect, but rigid quantitative/outcome framing in self-coached amateurs triggers documented self-criticism and abandonment loops.
- **Technique B (forward-looking outcome promise) partially translates under one narrow framing (low–moderate confidence):** its active ingredient is *response expectancy*, which the motor-learning literature shows is reliably induced by instruction/framing — even by a visual illusion — with **no coach present**, so it is more app-portable than the "coach gaze does the work" intuition assumes; but only a subjective, hard-to-disconfirm "you should feel more confident" framing is defensible. A capability guarantee ("you'll be a better passer") risks trust erosion and should not ship.
- **For both, the binding risk is not that the cognitive ingredient evaporates — it is that friction and failure modes swamp a small effect.** Ship a process-framed, reviewable session goal first; ship the confidence-framed promise only behind an A/B test; do not ship a capability-guarantee promise; treat the pair as theoretically coherent but unproven incrementally.

## Key Findings

The central mechanism question resolves differently for the two techniques, and the difference drives the verdicts.

**For Technique A, the coached-context effect is split between the goal act (cognitive, app-portable) and interpersonal accountability (largely lost in an app, partially rebuildable).** The cleanest decomposition — Epton, Currie & Armitage (2017, *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*; 141 papers, 384 effect sizes, N = 16,523) — found the *unique* effect of goal-setting net of other techniques is d = 0.34 (95% CI [0.28, 0.41]), with goals working best when difficult, **set publicly**, and group-based, and weaker evidence favoring **external monitoring by others** and **face-to-face delivery**. So roughly d ≈ 0.3–0.4 survives without a coach, but the amplifiers are exactly the interpersonal features an app strips out. The crucial encouraging finding is Harkin et al. (2016, *Psychological Bulletin*; 138 studies, N = 19,951): prompting progress monitoring raised goal attainment d⁺ = 0.40 (95% CI [0.32, 0.48]), and effects were **larger when progress was reported/made public and physically recorded** — and physical recording is exactly what an app does natively. The app can therefore rebuild part of the accountability amplifier it otherwise loses.

**For Technique B, the surprising and decision-relevant finding is that the active ingredient does not require an interpersonal coach at all.** The "forward-looking promise to the performer" is not the Pygmalion/coach-expectancy mechanism (which is interpersonal and genuinely does not translate). It is a *response-expectancy/placebo* mechanism directed at the performer's own anticipation, and the OPTIMAL-theory motor-learning literature has repeatedly produced enhanced-expectancy learning gains using framing alone — bogus normative feedback, a single opening verbal statement, an easier-looking "success" criterion, and even a visual illusion with no social agent. That makes Technique B's ingredient genuinely app-portable, contradicting the stakeholder intuition that coach presence carries it.

## Details

### Technique A — Athlete-Set Per-Session Goal

**Coached / controlled-context evidence (informative, but does NOT settle translation).** Williamson, Swann, Bennett, Bird, Goddard, Schweickle & Jackman (2022, *International Review of Sport and Exercise Psychology*, 17(2):1050–1078), meta-analysing 27 studies (1,764 participants, from 17,841 screened), found *process* goals had by far the largest performance effect (d = 1.36) versus performance goals (d = 0.44) and outcome goals (d = 0.09); specific (d = 0.37) and non-specific (d = 0.72) goals did not differ significantly. This is the single most important framing result for Technique A: **how-you-execute goals move performance; outcome goals barely do.** A 2024 Brigham Young University meta-analytic review (22 studies, competitive athletes) found goal-setting d = 0.38 (95% CI [0.15, 0.61]), with short-term and combined short+long-term goals beating long-term-only.

On the self-set question specifically, the controlled evidence is weaker than product intuition assumes: Locke & Latham's organizational work repeatedly found participatively-set versus assigned goals do **not** differ significantly when difficulty is held constant, and the Kyllo & Landers (1995) self-set advantage drew mostly on non-athletes. In actual skill-sport retention designs the effects are weak: Corrêa, de Souza & Santos (2006, *Perceptual and Motor Skills*; 44 female volleyball players, dig/forearm-pass-to-target) found **no significant between-group differences** across generic, specific long-term, specific short-term, and control goals — all groups improved. Corrêa & de Souza (2009, Bachman ladder, n = 100 novices) likewise found generic, difficult, easy, long/short, and self-set goals all produced similar motor learning. Shooting/closed-skill studies are more favorable (Boyce 1992, *JSEP*, n = 181: specific goals beat do-your-best; Boyce & Wayda 1994, *JSEP*: assigned and self-set both improved performance). **Net read: the coached-context skill-acquisition effect of goal *type* is real for process goals but modest and inconsistent — a caution against over-claiming.**

**Self-administered / translation evidence (this carries the verdict).** Hurley, Nezami, Sciamanna & Tate (2024, *Digital Health*), a secondary analysis of the LoseNow Physician Assisted trial (LNPA; NCT01606813; N = 550; UNC Chapel Hill & Penn State College of Medicine), found greater use of a personal goal-setting eHealth component was associated with improved weight loss at 6 months among active users — "a low-investment addition." But translation can be fragile: a JMIR mHealth weight-management RCT (N = 200, BMI > 25) found app-delivered implementation intentions produced **no benefit over an active control that also communicated cue-action links**, with benefit appearing only for poor planners — direct evidence that much of the apparent benefit is the *structure of the prompt*, not the personalization. The largest shipped self-coaching dataset is supportive: Duolingo's A/B tests showed decoupling the daily goal from the streak raised Day-14 retention 3.3% and DAU 1% (and increased the share of daily learners on a streak by 10.5% within 20 days); changing a button from "Continue" to "Commit to My Goal" significantly boosted engagement; and letting users *set their own* goal beat assigning one. User-set goals plus commitment language are retention-positive in self-administered settings.

**Failure modes (counterintuitive, decision-critical).** Bondaronek, Porter et al. (published 22 Oct 2025 in the *British Journal of Health Psychology*; UCL Institute of Health Informatics, University of Westminster, Loughborough University), an AI analysis of 58,881 tweets about the five top-grossing fitness apps (including MyFitnessPal, Strava, WW), documented that rigid, quantitative, algorithm-set goals produced shame, avoidance (deliberately not logging to escape negative feedback), and demoralization. Dr Paulina Bondaronek: "Self-monitoring and action planning are powerful behaviour change techniques. But we over-use them… We are good at blaming and shaming because we think it will help us to do better but actually it has the opposite effect." Epstein et al.'s lived-informatics work (CHI '16, UbiComp '15) independently documents goal-anchored self-tracking abandonment, with goal rigidity a named driver. **In self-coached amateurs — the population in scope — a poorly framed session goal becomes a recurring source of self-criticism rather than direction.**

### Technique B — Forward-Looking Outcome Promise

**The mechanism reframe is decisive.** Coach-expectancy (Pygmalion; Rosenthal & Jacobson 1968; Horn, Cox & Labrador 1998 in sport) works by changing the *coach's behavior* toward the athlete — that part genuinely does not translate to an app. But the promise *to the performer* is a response-expectancy manipulation, and Wulf & Lewthwaite's (2016, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*) OPTIMAL theory names "enhanced expectancies for future performance" as one of three independent learning factors, with a dopaminergic anticipation-of-reward mechanism. Critically, the experiments deliver expectancy by framing alone, with no coaching relationship:

- Palmer, Chiviacowsky & Wulf (2016, *Psychology of Sport and Exercise*; n = 34 students, golf putting): a larger "success" circle improved retention (η²p = .14) and transfer (η²p = .13).
- Pascua, Wulf & Lewthwaite (2015, *Journal of Sports Sciences*; n = 52): an enhanced-expectancy manipulation produced large effects on retention (η²p = .453) and transfer (η²p = .360).
- Wulf, Chiviacowsky & Lewthwaite (2012, *Psychology and Aging*; older women): **a single verbal statement at the start of practice** ("people like you usually perform well") enhanced self-efficacy and learning.
- Chauvel, Wulf & Maquestiaux (2015, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*): a **visual illusion** — no social agent whatsoever — made nongolfers perceive a larger hole, raising self-efficacy and retention.
- Crum & Langer (2007, *Psychological Science*): 84 female hotel room attendants across seven hotels; the group informed their cleaning work "is good exercise" and met Surgeon General recommendations showed, four weeks later, decreases in weight, blood pressure, body fat, waist-to-hip ratio, and BMI, despite no change in actual behavior.

**Boundary conditions and contested evidence (essential hedges).** Several studies failed to replicate enhanced-expectancy learning benefits (Ong, Lohse & Hodges 2015; Ong & Hodges 2019; Ziv, Ochayon & Lidor 2019; Cañal-Bruland et al. 2016); the apparent boundary condition is **"success with challenge"** — a too-easy or empty promise nulls the effect. Pascua et al. (2015) also found expectancy *alone* did not survive overnight forgetting (only expectancy plus an external-focus manipulation maintained retention). And almost all of this evidence is novices/students on novel lab tasks, so generalization to recreational skill-sport app users is an inferential step.

**Self-administered translation and failure modes.** Meditation apps already ship pre-session forward-looking framing (Headspace's "Set Your Daily Intention"), and Zawadzki, Torok, Peña & Gavrilova (2025, *Annals of Behavioral Medicine* 59(1):kaaf025) — an RCT of 138 non-faculty employees (M age 38.19; 75.36% female) at UC Merced, Headspace 10 min/day versus wait-list, using ecological momentary assessment over 8 weeks — found real stress reduction (with ~50% attrition), an existence proof that framing-heavy self-administered sessions produce measured effects. **No direct A/B test of neutral-description versus forward-looking-confidence versus capability-guarantee framing in a skill-sport app was located — the central evidentiary gap for Technique B.** On the downside, promise-breaking literature (trust-game neuroeconomics; brand-trust research) and HCI work on AI trust (Dhuliawala et al. 2023, where even a few confidently-wrong outputs damaged user trust with slow recovery) all show unmet promises damage trust durably. A directly falsifiable promise is therefore dangerous; a subjective, self-fulfilling one is safe precisely because the user's own expectancy tends to make it true and it is hard to disconfirm.

### The Pair (A + B Together)

Theory favors **mutual reinforcement, not competition.** OPTIMAL theory treats enhanced expectancy and goal-directed attention as additive, independent contributors, and Pascua et al. (2015) directly showed enhanced expectancy + external focus were additive (no interaction) on retention; Zimmerman's self-regulated-learning model places goal-setting and self-efficacy/expectancy together in the same forethought phase. Pre-performance-routine evidence — which bundles a goal/cue with expectancy/self-talk — is favorable: Rupprecht, Tran & Gröpel (2021, *International Review of Sport and Exercise Psychology*, 17(1):39–64) found an experimental low-pressure effect of Hedges' g = 0.64 (95% CI [0.45, 0.83]), not moderated by skill level (so novices benefit). **However**, a tenpin-bowling field study found combining routine components yielded no greater effect than a single component in isolation, and the HCI literature shows session-start friction is itself an abandonment driver. **Verdict on the pair: weakly supported / neutral** — theoretically coherent and not contraindicated, but the incremental lift of adding B to A is unproven and may be offset by surface clutter. Ship A first; A/B-test the addition of B.

### Practitioner Intuition (separated from peer-reviewed evidence)

Sport-psychology practitioners and SMART-goal coaching curricula treat session-start goals and a positive forward-looking mindset as standard and assume they are self-administrable, and they stress routine *review/revision* to prevent automaticity — consistent with the OPTIMAL evidence and supporting the end-of-session-review recommendation, but this is authority, not data. The opposing practitioner camp (accountability-coaching services) argues the *human* check-in is the irreplaceable ingredient; the Harkin (2016) finding that *recorded* monitoring substitutes for part of the human role partially rebuts this.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Ship now: process-framed, user-set session goal with end-of-session review.**
1. Frame as a *process/learning* goal, never an outcome target: *"Pick one thing to work on this session — e.g., 'keep my platform stable on every pass.'"* (Grounded in Williamson 2022: process d = 1.36 vs outcome d = 0.09.)
2. Make it user-set from a constrained menu (autonomy + reduced blank-page friction; supported by Duolingo's user-set-goal win).
3. Close the loop at session end: *"Last session you worked on platform stability — how did it feel today?"* This rebuilds the monitoring amplifier the app otherwise loses (Harkin 2016: recorded progress raises attainment).
4. Avoid rigid quantitative targets and streak-style punishment (directly implicated in the self-criticism/abandonment loop; Bondaronek 2025).

**Stage 2 — A/B-test, don't default-ship: the forward-looking promise.** Use only the subjective framing: *"Today we're focusing on passing. By the end, you should feel more confident with this skill."* Never ship a capability guarantee. Pair it with a session difficulty calibrated for "success with challenge," since a trivially easy session nulls the effect (Ong/Ziv boundary condition; practitioner-inference label on the calibration detail).

**Stage 3 — substitute for lost social accountability via app surfaces:** physically-recorded session logs, optional training-partner visibility, and end-of-session reflection against the stated goal. Harkin (2016) shows recorded/public monitoring is the partial substitute for the human monitor.

**Thresholds that would change these recommendations:**
- If an in-app A/B test shows the confidence promise *reduces* 4-week retention or self-reported enjoyment versus control, **pull it** — the trust-erosion/fluff risk has materialized.
- If process-goal users show rising self-criticism signals (declining logging, negative free-text sentiment) versus control, **soften framing further or make the goal optional.**
- If the A+B arm does not beat the A-only arm on engagement or retention, **do not ship B** — the clutter cost is not being repaid.

## Caveats

- **Population mismatch is the dominant limitation.** Almost all quantitative evidence comes from elite athletes, student novices on novel lab tasks, or health-behavior populations — not adult recreational skill-sport athletes with 2–5 years' experience training 1–3×/week self-coached. The verdicts lean on the self-administered/app body of evidence (Duolingo, JMIR, fitness-app post-mortems, Headspace) for the translation question and use the coached/lab body only for mechanism. Effect-size magnitudes should not be assumed to transfer at face value.
- **The enhanced-expectancy effect is real but contested**, with multiple non-replications and a "success-with-challenge" boundary condition; expectancy alone may not survive overnight without an accompanying attentional manipulation.
- **No direct, in-population A/B test exists** comparing neutral vs confidence-promise vs capability-guarantee framing in a skill-sport app — the single study that would most change the answer is a 3-arm (optionally 4-arm) RCT, ~N = 240 recreational racquet/court-sport adults (2–5 yrs, 1–3×/week, self-coached), randomized to (a) process session-goal + end-of-session review, (b) forward-looking confidence promise, (c) quick-start control, (d optional) A+B, measuring within-session engagement, blinded 6-week skill retention, and 12-week adherence, with self-criticism/affect (PANAS) and promise-fulfilment trust as mediators, powered for d = 0.30.
- Several practitioner and trade sources cited for context (accountability-coaching claims, app design write-ups) are not peer-reviewed and are labeled as such; they inform but do not drive the verdicts.
