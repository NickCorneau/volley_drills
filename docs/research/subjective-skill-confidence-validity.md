# Construct Validity Of Weekly Self-Rated Skill Confidence (Adult Recreational Skill-Sport)

## Agent Quick Scan

- **Bottom line (3-vendor convergence under a favorable-to-skeptical spread).** A weekly single-item self-rated skill-confidence reading measures **state self-efficacy / felt readiness**, **not** a clean longitudinal index of actual skill development. It should **not** be surfaced as "your skill is improving." All three vendors agree on this even though they disagree on how usable confidence is at all: the variance in a weekly confidence score is dominated by **last-session outcome / recency, mood, and fatigue/soreness**, not by the slow, often sub-perceptual accumulation of motor skill at 1-3 sessions/week.
- **The spectrum (this is the vendor-number axis).** **Vendor 1 (most favorable):** acceptable but *highly conditional* validity — confidence **can** be deployed as a user-facing longitudinal trace **if** the architecture mitigates confounds and pairs it with a behavioral proxy. **Vendor 2 (middle):** *not* valid as the **sole** proxy — ship it only as a clearly-labeled **secondary** trend behind a behavioral primary. **Vendor 3 (most skeptical):** weak and confounded — do **not** make it primary (stated **moderate-to-high** confidence on the negative claim); use a behavioral proxy as the headline and demote confidence to a secondary reflection trace.
- **Unanimous mechanics.** (1) A weekly single item = **state**, not trait. (2) Single-item self-efficacy can be **psychometrically adequate** (single-item ↔ multi-item r ≈ .63-.80; ICCs ~.82-.88 in some sport contexts) — reliability is **not** the blocker; **construct contamination** is. (3) **Directional biases** hit the 2-5-year intermediate squarely: novice/intermediate **over**-confidence (Dunning-Kruger / "beginner's bubble") **and** plateau-blindness **under**-detecting slow implicit gains. (4) Therefore a **confidence dip can signal a metacognitive breakthrough** (vendor 1's "paradox of progress"), not a training failure — never auto-read a dip as degradation. (5) Item must be **skill-specific** and **present-state "can-do"**, never global ("overall game") and never a retrospective "since last week" change-anchor; the **backend** computes the week-over-week delta. (6) **Pair with a behavioral proxy** and treat divergence (high practice / flat confidence) as **coachable**, churn-preventing. (7) Population is **under-researched**; every quantitative claim is extrapolation — **instrument to learn**.
- **The one real disagreement: scale format.** Vendor 1 → **100-pt VAS or 11-pt NRS** (VAS for micro-change sensitivity; 5-pt Likert creates artificial step-function plateaus). Vendor 2 → **7-pt fully-labeled Likert** (VAS adds friction with no reliability gain). Vendor 3 → **5- or 11-pt Likert, avoid VAS**. The only format inside **all three** acceptable sets is an **11-point NRS (0-10)**, skill-specific, present-state framed, fully verbalized anchors where feasible, fixed weekly cadence administered **off** the immediate post-session window.
- **What it changes in this repo.** Refines and **strengthens** the sibling [`single-tracked-metric`](./single-tracked-metric-amateur-skill-apps.md) note's "confidence is a contested skill-side metric" conclusion, and refines the **M002 "Weekly Confidence Loop"** framing: confidence is a defensible **felt-readiness / engagement / churn-risk** signal, but **not** an objective skill readout, and can move **opposite** to skill during intermediate plateaus. Gates `O23`'s forward-looking outcome promise (frame the payoff as *felt readiness*, never *proof you improved*, and never promise a confidence rise). Feeds `O21`(b)/(c): if captured, confidence is a per-skill **state trace**, not tracked proficiency. Reinforces the `D91` cohort-instrumentation case.
- **Scope of this note.** Vendor 1, vendor 2, vendor 3 all folded in (all received 2026-06-02). 3-of-N state; structure accommodates more responses without rewriting.

## Executive conclusion

Three independent vendor memos arrive at a **shared decision-relevant answer through a favorable-to-skeptical spread**. None located a study measuring a weekly single-item skill-confidence reading in the exact target population (self-coached adult recreational, 2-5 years, 1-3 sessions/week, non-competitive); all three build their answer from adjacent literatures (sport-confidence/self-efficacy, single-item measurement science, EMA, motor learning, HCI self-tracking) and flag the extrapolation.

- **Vendor 1 (most favorable):** answers the core question "in the affirmative, provided the application architecture explicitly accounts for the metric's volatility." Confidence is **state self-efficacy** — behaviorally predictive of adherence and persistence — and is **deployable as a user-facing trace** if paired with a behavioral proxy and read through the paradox-of-progress lens. It is explicitly **not** a 1:1 map of motor learning.
- **Vendor 2 (middle):** "the best evidence does **not** support using weekly confidence as the **sole** longitudinal proxy." It has some validity, but mostly for **perceived capability / readiness**. Ship it, if at all, as a **secondary** subjective trend; make **skill-specific completed practice/drill volume** the primary behavioral signal, ideally in a hybrid (behavioral headline + periodic behavioral benchmark + confidence as context).
- **Vendor 3 (most skeptical):** "weak and confounded construct validity… should **not** be shipped as the **primary** user-facing skill-progress signal" (moderate-to-high confidence on the negative). Behavioral proxy primary; confidence demoted to a **secondary, explicitly-framed reflection trace** shown **beside** the behavioral trace so divergence becomes a coachable feature.

**The convergence is the decision.** Read as a band: confidence is a **valid, reasonably reliable signal of felt readiness / state self-efficacy**, and a genuinely useful **engagement and churn-risk** indicator — but it is **not** a trustworthy standalone readout of objective skill change for this population, and it is **directionally biased** in exactly the 2-5-year band the product targets. Five independent arrows, each cited by at least two vendors, point the same way:

1. **State, not trait, not motor learning.** A single weekly item is a compressed **state self-efficacy** read (Vealey's state vs trait split; Bandura's four efficacy sources). For self-coached amateurs, two of Bandura's four sources (verbal persuasion from a coach, curated vicarious experience) are structurally missing, so the rating **over-indexes** on the most recent enactive experience plus current physiological state (vendor 1).
2. **Confounds swamp signal at this cadence.** EMA work puts **within-person variance in state self-efficacy at 51-89%** (vendor 1); a single training session's feedback quality explains **~17% of within-athlete confidence variance** (Carpentier & Mageau, N=49, 15 sessions — vendor 2); confidence **tracks the last outcome, not the skill** (Gagnon-Dolbec lacrosse N=50: confidence rose/fell with win/loss feedback while performance stayed stable — vendor 3); fatigue/soreness produce large state swings (post-match mornings 35-40% worse on self-rated fatigue/soreness — vendor 3). Meanwhile true weekly skill change is **small and slow** (motor consolidation over ~5-10 spaced sessions — vendor 3).
3. **Directional bias in the target band.** Novice/intermediate **over**-confidence (Sanchez & Dunning 2018 "beginner's bubble," 6 studies; Kruger & Dunning 1999; Dunning-Kruger confirmed in sport coaches N=94 — vendors 2 & 3) **and** plateau-blindness / the "paradox of progress" (a structured-football RCT, N=105, found the **control** group reported *higher* self-efficacy because real skill gains heightened self-critical awareness — vendor 1). Net: transitions over-read, steady-state intermediates under-read true slow gains. A **confidence dip can mean the athlete just learned enough to see their own flaws** — never auto-interpret it as failure.
4. **Framing determines whether it holds.** Skill-specific present-state "can-do" framing has the best validity (concordance/specificity; Moritz et al. self-efficacy↔performance r ≈ .38, task-specific efficacy up to 52% of performance variance). **Global** "overall game" framing collapses (forced averaging). **Retrospective "since last week"** change-anchors collapse worst (recall + present-state contamination; manufacture a progress narrative). The **backend** must compute the delta from absolute weekly readings.
5. **Behavioral proxies are complementary, and the honest primary.** Practice volume has a modest-but-real, mood-immune link to skill (deliberate practice ≈ 18% of sports-performance variance; r ≈ .48 in music) and is **stickier** than self-report (HCI abandonment evidence). All three vendors recommend **pairing** confidence with a behavioral proxy; vendors 2 & 3 make the behavioral proxy primary; vendor 1 makes confidence deployable **only** alongside one. Divergence (high practice / flat confidence) is the **plateau signal** worth surfacing.

**Practical implication for this repo:** do not ship weekly confidence as the user-facing *skill-progress* signal. If confidence appears, frame it as **felt readiness / confidence in a specific skill**, pair it with a behavioral receipt, compute trends in the backend, never promise a weekly rise, and never auto-read a dip as failure. The honest objective-skill upgrade everyone points toward is a **repeated structured drill score on a focal skill** (the same conclusion the sibling single-tracked-metric note reached).

## Reconciliation

Three vendors on a favorable-to-skeptical spectrum. The reconciliation matters because the product posture must be robust across all three positions.

### Where the vendors agree (high-confidence takeaways)

- **No direct measurement exists** for the exact target population; all three answers are extrapolation, explicitly flagged.
- **A weekly single item measures state self-efficacy / felt readiness**, not trait and not a 1:1 map of motor learning. Strong 3/3.
- **Confidence is NOT a clean objective skill-development readout.** Vendor 1 says "does not, and cannot, measure the absolute aggregation of motor pathways"; vendor 2 says "weak primary, useful secondary"; vendor 3 says "weak and confounded." Direction is unanimous; only the *severity* differs.
- **Dominant confound order: last-session outcome / recency → mood → fatigue/soreness.** Strong 3/3.
- **Two directional biases hit the 2-5-yr band:** intermediate **over**-confidence (Dunning-Kruger / beginner's bubble) and plateau-blindness (**under**-detecting slow implicit gains). A confidence **dip can mean a breakthrough** (vendor 1's paradox of progress), so never auto-read a dip as degradation. Strong 3/3.
- **Item must be skill-specific + present-state "can-do."** Global "overall game" collapses; retrospective "since last week" change-anchor collapses worst; backend computes the delta. Strong 3/3.
- **Pair with a behavioral proxy; treat divergence as coachable.** All three recommend pairing; the divergence (high practice / flat confidence) is the plateau signal worth surfacing to prevent churn. Strong 3/3.
- **Population under-researched → instrument to learn.** All three call for an internal longitudinal study pairing weekly confidence + objective skill criterion + mood/fatigue covariates. Strong 3/3.

## Synthesis stability — what would change this

Evaluate future evidence (a vendor 4, a direct study, or internal cohort data) against these pre-registered bars rather than re-litigating the synthesis.

- **Bar 1 — would flip to "confidence IS a defensible standalone skill proxy."** A longitudinal field study in the target population (self-coached adult recreational, 2-5 yr, 1-3 sessions/wk) showing **within-person weekly confidence tracks an objective skill criterion** (e.g., serve-in %, grade sent, validated drill score) **after partialling out last-session outcome, mood, and fatigue**, with the residual confidence↔skill coupling materially positive. Nothing in the current evidence approaches this; all three vendors expect the opposite.
- **Bar 2 — would recenter the design without flipping the posture.** (a) Format evidence settling VAS vs 11-pt NRS vs Likert for this dose/cadence — update the recommended format, keep the posture. (b) A validated **UI debiasing** intervention (e.g., pre-rating "ignore soreness" prompt) that demonstrably reduces confound variance — adopt the mitigation. (c) Evidence that a low-burden confidence variant tracks objective skill — strengthen confidence's role toward co-primary.
- **Bar 3 — would narrow / complicate scope.** Published **gender / age / sport-specific bias magnitudes** at the target population, or a measured **temporal duration** of the paradox-of-progress / plateau dip — add subpopulation handling and timing rules; do not change the headline posture.
- **Bar 4 — would prompt internal measurement, not a literature update.** Any vendor reinforcing that the literature has hit its ceiling here (all three effectively do). Next quality-increasing move is **instrumenting the product's own cohort** (`D91`, scaffolded on [`pre-telemetry-validation-protocol`](./pre-telemetry-validation-protocol.md)): log weekly confidence + an objective skill proxy + mood/fatigue covariates so the missing variance-partitioning study gets built internally.

### Stability check: where this synthesis could be fragile

1. **No direct measurement.** The entire synthesis is inference from adjacent literatures; a single well-designed target-population study would outweigh the current stack.
2. **Severity, not direction, is the disagreement.** If a vendor 4 argued confidence is a *clean* skill signal, it would have to clear Bar 1 — none of the three came close, so the directional posture is robust even though the severity band is wide.
3. **Format recommendation is the weakest-held element.** VAS is genuinely contested (1 pro, 2 against). The 11-pt NRS overlap is a reconciliation, not a measured optimum; treat it as provisional pending Bar 2 evidence.

## Decision implications

- **M002 "Weekly Confidence Loop" framing (active planning).** This evidence does **not** kill confidence for M002, but it **constrains what confidence is allowed to mean**. Keep weekly confidence as a low-burden **felt-readiness / engagement** half of the receipt, paired with the adherence anchor the sibling note settled on (**planned-vs-completed session ratio**). Frame the confidence number as *"how confident you feel in [skill]"* — **never** as *"your skill improved this week."* Compute trends in the backend over rolling windows; **never** display a single-week delta as signal; **never** auto-read a dip as failure. The milestone **name** bakes confidence in as the skill signal — all three vendors say it is not — so reframe the *meaning* (felt readiness, not skill proof) even if the name persists, and leave a clean seam for the objective upgrade (a repeated structured drill score on a focal skill).
- **Reconciliation with the sibling [`single-tracked-metric`](./single-tracked-metric-amateur-skill-apps.md) note (additive, this file only).** The sibling note concluded weekly confidence is a **contested skill-side metric** — "a defensible low-burden v1 proxy for *readiness*, NOT objective learning" — with one vendor pro, one mixed, one anti. **This confidence-validity evidence STRENGTHENS that conclusion and sharpens its mechanism.** It strengthens it by showing the skepticism is not a one-vendor outlier: even the **most favorable** vendor here forbids the objective-skill claim and requires a behavioral pairing. It sharpens it by naming *why* confidence is unsafe as a skill signal specifically for this population — it is dominated by recency/mood/fatigue (51-89% within-person variance) and **directionally biased** in the 2-5-yr band, such that confidence can move **opposite** to skill during intermediate plateaus (the paradox of progress). Net effect on the sibling's "keep confidence as a readiness proxy, plan the objective drill-score upgrade" recommendation: **strengthened, not weakened.** It also adds a genuine *positive* the sibling under-weighted: confidence is a real and useful **engagement / churn-risk** signal (vendor 1's strongest point, conceded by all three) — so the right move is to **keep it but relabel it**, not to drop it. For the M002 "Weekly Confidence Loop" framing specifically, both notes now point the same way: confidence is fine as a felt-readiness half of a two-metric pair, but the name overstates its epistemic role and the receipt should not let it carry the objective-skill claim.
- **`O23` (forward-looking outcome promise) — gate resolution input.** `O23` asks whether the loop should carry a "by the end of practice you should feel more confident in this skill" promise, and the brief explicitly gated it on confidence being "a defensible readout, not noise." Resolution: a promise framed as a **feeling / readiness** outcome ("you should feel more ready / more confident in [skill]") is **defensible** — confidence is a valid felt-readiness construct. A promise framed as **proof of skill gain** ("you will have improved") is **not** supported by confidence alone. Critically, the paradox-of-progress means an honest promise must **not guarantee a confidence increase**, because intermediates can dip while genuinely improving — so any promise should (a) be framed as felt readiness, not skill proof; (b) avoid promising a week-over-week confidence rise; (c) be backed by a behavioral receipt so a flat/declining confidence week is reframed (normalized non-linear learning), not read as failure.
- **`O21`(b)/(c) (per-skill state mechanism + storage shape) — input only.** If confidence is captured, it is a per-skill **state self-efficacy trace**, not tracked proficiency — store it as such (skill-keyed, weekly, with mood/fatigue context where feasible), and do not let it stand in for the per-skill proficiency vector that `O21` debates. Confidence is a weak observation channel for proficiency; if used to *establish* per-skill state, it must be combined with completion/score signal, never relied on alone. (Storage-vector vs scalar choice itself remains governed by `O21` and [`skill-correlation-amateur-beach`](./skill-correlation-amateur-beach.md).)
- **`D91` cohort instrumentation.** Log weekly confidence **and** an objective skill proxy **and** mood/fatigue/last-outcome covariates from day one, so the within-person confidence↔skill coupling study that does not exist gets built internally and the 2026-07-20 re-eval reads real dose-response signal rather than extrapolation. Consistent with `D124`/`D130` staging.

## Gaps

1. **No study measures a weekly single-item skill-confidence item in the exact target population** (adult, self-coached, recreational, 2-5 yr, 1-3 sessions/wk, non-competitive, non-clinical). Every quantitative claim here is extrapolated from competitive/elite sport-confidence, organizational single-item, clinical self-efficacy, motor-learning, or HCI literatures.
2. **No effect-size decomposition** of confound variance vs true-skill variance in weekly confidence at this cadence.
3. **No documented case** of a consumer app shipping then killing a literal self-rated *skill-confidence* feature — the design argument is by analogy (Duolingo removed adjacent subjective proxies; mood/music apps treat self-rating as context, not progress).
4. **No published gender / age / sport-specific bias magnitudes** at the target population.
5. **Temporal duration of the paradox-of-progress / plateau dip** is unquantified — how many weeks an intermediate spends in suppressed, hyper-critical confidence before objective skill forces a positive re-evaluation.
6. **Optimal in-UI debiasing interventions** lack peer-reviewed A/B testing.

**Study that would close them:** a 12-16-week longitudinal multilevel field study of 2-5-year recreational players logging **weekly skill-specific confidence** + **weekly mood/fatigue/last-session outcome** + **weekly practice volume** + a **periodic objective behavioral benchmark** (serve-in %, grade sent, validated drill score), computing within-person confidence↔skill coupling and confound partial-correlations; paired with (a) a **format RCT** (5-pt vs 7-pt vs 11-pt vs VAS vs change-anchor weekly test-retest ICC) and (b) an **in-app A/B test** of behavioral-primary vs confidence-primary progress readouts on retention and calibration. The `D91` cohort instrumentation above can begin to approximate this.
